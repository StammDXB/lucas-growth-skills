# Lens: Voice and Tone

Load when: writing in a defined brand voice, defining one, or diagnosing why
copy does not sound like the brand.

## The distinction people get wrong

**Voice is constant. Tone flexes.**

Voice is who the brand is: it does not change between a launch announcement and
an outage apology. Tone is how that voice modulates for the situation: the same
person is recognisably themselves at a funeral and at a party, but they do not
sound identical.

Most "our voice is inconsistent" complaints are actually tone failures. The
brand used its launch register for a support message. Diagnose which layer broke
before rewriting anything.

## The measurement instrument

Nielsen Norman Group's four tone dimensions. Each is a spectrum, not a binary,
and a brand sits somewhere on all four.

| Dimension | Spectrum |
|---|---|
| Humour | Funny to serious |
| Formality | Formal to casual |
| Respectfulness | Respectful to irreverent |
| Enthusiasm | Enthusiastic to matter-of-fact |

Source: Nielsen Norman Group, "The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice."

Why this beats adjectives: "friendly but professional" is unfalsifiable and
every brand claims it. A position on four spectrums is checkable. Two people can
disagree about whether a draft sits at 2 or 4 on formality, and that argument is
productive. Nobody can argue productively about "authentic."

**Applied:** when a brand voice is supplied as adjectives, convert it to
positions on these four before writing. When none is supplied, ask for the four
or infer them from samples and state your inference.

## Capturing a voice from existing material

When the brand has no documented voice but has published work, reverse-engineer
it rather than inventing one.

1. Collect 5 to 10 real samples across contexts.
2. Score each on the four dimensions. Note the spread. A wide spread means the
   voice is not yet consistent, which is itself the finding.
3. Extract the vocabulary: words that recur, words conspicuously absent.
4. Note sentence architecture: average length, variance, whether it uses
   fragments, questions, contractions, second person.
5. Write the "we are / we are not" pairs. The second column does more work than
   the first, because it rules things out.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Direct | Blunt |
| Warm | Chummy |
| Expert | Academic |

Each pair must exclude something a reasonable writer might otherwise do. "We are
professional, we are not unprofessional" is a wasted row.

## Tone modulation by context

The same voice at different settings. Define these before writing, because the
defaults are what drift.

| Context | Typical shift |
|---|---|
| Marketing and launch | Enthusiasm up |
| Product and UI | Formality down, enthusiasm down, brevity up |
| Support and help | Respectfulness up, humour down |
| Error and failure | Humour down hard, matter-of-fact up |
| Outage or apology | All four toward serious, formal, respectful, matter-of-fact |
| Internal | Formality down, directness up |

The most common failure is humour in an error state. The user is already
frustrated; a joke reads as the brand not taking their problem seriously.

## Holding voice across a piece

- **Read the draft aloud.** Anywhere you stumble is a voice break.
- **Check the seams.** Voice drifts at transitions, in the middle third, and in
  boilerplate near the end. Openings get the most attention and drift least.
- **The substitution test.** Swap a competitor's name in. If the copy still
  reads as natural and true, the voice is not doing any work. This is the
  commutation test from `semiotics.md` applied to language.

## Gotchas

- **Adjective voices.** "Bold, human, innovative" is not a voice. It is three
  words every brand in the category also claims. Convert to spectrums.
- **Voice as decoration.** Voice is not adding personality on top of finished
  copy. If the argument is wrong, a better voice makes it worse, because it makes
  the wrong thing more persuasive.
- **Register drift under pressure.** Long pieces drift toward neutral corporate
  register as they go. Check the last third specifically.
- **Uniform rhythm.** Sentence-length uniformity is the strongest tell of
  machine-written text, stronger than any word or punctuation mark. A defined
  voice includes a rhythm, and rhythm means variance.
- **Borrowed voice.** Copying an admired brand's voice produces a brand that
  sounds like a tribute act. Take the method, not the output.

## Honest gaps

The NN/g four-dimension model is well established as a practitioner instrument.
No controlled study demonstrating that a specific position on these dimensions
causes better commercial outcomes was located. Treat the model as a tool for
consistency and diagnosis, not as evidence that a particular voice performs
better.
