# Campaign Craft: Insight to Platform to Execution

Load when: developing a campaign idea, territory, or platform.

## The distinction everything rests on

**An idea is not an execution, and neither is a tagline.**

- **Execution:** one artifact. A film, a poster, a post.
- **Tagline:** a line of words. Often confused with the idea because it is the most visible part.
- **Platform idea:** a *thought* that generates many executions across years and channels without repeating itself.

The test: can you describe it without describing any specific asset? If the only
way to explain it is "there's a film where...", it is an execution. If it
generates a brief for a film, an event, a product decision, and a hiring page, it
is a platform.

Second test: could it still be running in three years with entirely different
executions and still be recognisably the same idea?

## The sequence

**Insight → Proposition → Platform → Executions**

Most weak work skips to executions, then reverse-engineers a proposition to
justify them. You can spot this because the proposition sounds like a
description of the ad.

### 1. Insight

An insight is not an observation and not a data point. It is a **tension the
audience recognises but has not articulated**, which the brand is positioned to
resolve.

Weak: "Busy parents have limited time." That is an observation. Everyone knows it.

Stronger: "Parents will happily spend two hours cooking something their kids
refuse to eat, because the effort is the apology for not being there."

Tests:
- Would the audience say "how did you know that" rather than "yes obviously"?
- Is there tension in it, something in conflict?
- Does it implicate the brand, or would it work for any brand in the category?
- Is it uncomfortable? Insights that flatter the audience are usually observations.

### 2. Proposition

One sentence stating what the brand offers against that tension. It is written
for the team, not the audience: it is not copy.

The discipline is subtraction. A proposition carrying two ideas produces work
that does neither.

**Honest note on terminology:** "single-minded proposition" is standard agency
vocabulary, but the Account Planning Group's own explainer does not define it,
and no authoritative published definition was located. Use the concept, do not
cite it as a formalised framework.

### 3. Platform

The organising thought. It must be:
- **Generative.** Produces ideas rather than needing them.
- **Ownable.** Fails the substitution test if a competitor's name is dropped in.
- **Durable.** Survives a change of channel, a change of season, and a change of CMO.
- **Livable.** The company can actually behave this way, not just say it.

### 4. Executions

Now, and only now, the artifacts. Each should be recognisably from the platform
and should not be a restatement of the others.

## The AI failure mode, and it is not what you expect

This is the highest-value item in this reference, because it tells you to do the
opposite of the obvious thing.

The failure of AI-generated campaign ideas is **not** that they are generic.
Si, Yang and Hashimoto found LLM-generated research ideas were judged **more
novel** than expert-generated ideas (p < 0.05). Doshi and Hauser found that
AI-assisted outputs were **more similar to each other**.

So the problem is **diversity collapse across the set**, not weakness of any
single idea.

**Consequence:** "push for more novelty" optimises the wrong variable. Any single
idea will read as fresh. The set will silently converge on one region of the
solution space.

**The protocol that addresses it:** generate under **deliberately divergent
constraints** rather than generating more.

Before generating, fix three to five *incompatible* constraints, and produce one
territory under each:

- One that is only true for this brand and no competitor
- One that would work with no budget
- One that would only work with a large budget
- One that starts from the product's biggest weakness
- One that a risk-averse client would reject
- One that requires the company to change something operationally

Then check **variance across the set**, not the quality of the best one. If three
territories could be merged without loss, you generated one idea three times.

## Pressure-testing an idea

- **Substitution.** Put a competitor's name on it. Still works? Not ownable.
- **The three-year test.** Could this still be running in three years?
- **The brief test.** Write a brief for a second execution from it. If you cannot, it is an execution wearing a platform's clothes.
- **The operations test.** What would the company have to do differently on Monday? If nothing, it is decoration.
- **The disagreement test.** Could an intelligent colleague argue against it? Ideas nobody can oppose are usually category conventions restated.

## Gotchas

- **Proposition written as copy.** If it sounds like a tagline, it is doing the
  wrong job. Propositions are internal.
- **Insight that is really a segment description.** Demographics are not tension.
- **Platform that is a theme.** "Innovation" and "Togetherness" are subjects, not
  ideas. They generate nothing because they exclude nothing.
- **The idea that only works with the art.** If the concept collapses when
  described in words, it is an execution.
- **Reverse-engineered strategy.** Executions first, proposition retrofitted.
  Diagnostic: the proposition describes the ad rather than the offer.
