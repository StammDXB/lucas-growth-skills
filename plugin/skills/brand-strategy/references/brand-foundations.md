# Lens: Brand Foundations

Load when: working on positioning, brand architecture, distinctive assets, or
any question of what a brand is and how it grows.

## The live disagreement you must navigate, not resolve

Two well-evidenced schools directly contradict each other. Pretending they
agree produces incoherent strategy.

**Differentiation school** (Ries and Trout, Reeves). Brands grow by occupying a
distinct position in the mind. Find the gap, own it, communicate it relentlessly.

**Distinctiveness school** (Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass). Buyers are not loyal,
categories are bought habitually, and brands grow by being **easy to notice and
easy to buy** rather than meaningfully different. Sharp: "throw that Unique
Selling Proposition out the door. Because customers don't care."

**How to choose:**

| Lean differentiation when | Lean distinctiveness when |
|---|---|
| Considered, high-involvement purchase | Habitual, low-involvement purchase |
| Buyer actively compares options | Buyer barely thinks about the category |
| Small brand entering an occupied category | Large brand defending penetration |
| B2B with a documented evaluation process | FMCG, retail, mass consumer |
| Genuine functional difference exists | Products are near-parity |

**State which you are applying and why.** A strategy that quietly mixes both
produces a positioning statement nobody can act on.

## Distinctive assets

The elements that let a buyer identify the brand without reading the name:
colour, shape, character, sonic, typeface, layout, mascot, phrase.

They are about **recognition speed**, not meaning. An asset works when it is
uniquely linked to your brand in the buyer's memory, and its value is destroyed
by inconsistency far faster than it is built by brilliance.

The practical implication most brands get wrong: **changing them resets the
asset.** A rebrand that refreshes the colour palette every three years never
accumulates recognition. Continuity is the asset.

## Category entry points

The cues that trigger category consideration: occasions, needs, moods, times,
places. "Something for a long drive." "Something to bring to a dinner."

Growth comes from being mentally available across **more** entry points, not
from being more loved at one. This reframes the brief: the question is not "how
do we make them love us more" but "in how many buying situations do we come to
mind at all."

Ehrenberg-Bass work. Well evidenced in FMCG and mass categories. Treat
generalisation to niche B2B as unproven.

## Positioning, written usefully

A positioning statement earns its place only if it excludes things. Test it by
asking what it forbids. If nothing, it is a description.

The components that matter:
- **For whom**, specifically enough to exclude people
- **Against what alternative**, including doing nothing
- **What you claim**, in one idea
- **Why it is believable**, the reason to believe
- **What you give up.** The most-skipped and most-diagnostic component. A
  position with no sacrifice is a wish list.

**The sacrifice test is the fastest diagnostic in brand work.** Ask what the
brand is deliberately not for, and not good at. If the answer is nothing, there
is no position.

## Brand architecture

- **Branded house.** One master brand, sub-brands as descriptors. Efficient,
  concentrated risk, constrains how far the portfolio can stretch.
- **House of brands.** Independent brands, quiet parent. Expensive, isolates
  risk, allows contradictory positions to coexist.
- **Endorsed.** Sub-brand leads, parent lends credibility.

The decision is usually driven by risk isolation and audience overlap, not by
aesthetics. Ask: would a scandal in one damage the others? Do the audiences
overlap enough to share equity?

## Brand equity, honestly

Equity is the premium in price, preference, or resilience that the brand earns
beyond the product's functional merit.

**Be careful here.** Equity measurement is contested, brand valuation
methodologies vary widely and are often commissioned by parties with an
interest, and league-table brand values are not a reliable input to strategy.

What is defensible: track price premium sustained, unprompted awareness within
the category, and share of preference among category buyers. Treat any single
composite "brand value" number with suspicion.

## Gotchas

- **Positioning that excludes nothing.** The most common failure. It reads well
  and forbids nothing.
- **Values mistaken for positioning.** "Integrity, innovation, excellence" is an
  internal culture statement, not a market position. Every competitor claims the
  same three.
- **Confusing the two schools.** Applying distinctiveness logic to a considered
  B2B purchase, or differentiation logic to a parity FMCG product.
- **Rebranding as a substitute for repositioning.** New identity, same position,
  no change in what the company is for.
- **Distinctive assets refreshed on a cycle.** Destroys the asset it was meant to
  build.
- **Archetype as strategy.** Archetypes are a useful vocabulary for tone. They
  impose a fixed universal typology after the fact and do not tell you what the
  brand is for. Do not mistake picking one for having positioned.
