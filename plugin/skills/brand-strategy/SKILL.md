# Brand Strategy

Positioning is a decision about what you give up. Everything else follows from
that, and most brand work fails because that decision was never made.

## Before anything: size the ask

If this is a quick question, a one-line lookup, a terminology check, or a small
task carrying no real stakes, answer it directly and stop. No clarifying
sequence, no rubric, no delivery note, no assumptions block.

The steps below are for real deliverables. Run them on a small question and you
produce ceremony instead of judgment, which is worse than a plain answer.

## Step 1: Clarify before positioning

Do not produce positioning from a thin brief. Ask if these are missing.

Required:
1. **What does the business actually sell**, and to whom, at what price?
2. **Who are the real alternatives**, including doing nothing?
3. **What is demonstrably true** about this business that competitors cannot say?
4. **What is the business willing to give up?** If nothing, there is no position
   to find and you should say so.
5. **What is the actual problem?** "We need a rebrand" usually means something
   else. Growth stalled, sales cannot explain the offer, or the team disagrees
   internally are three different problems with three different fixes.

Never invent market data, competitor claims, or audience research.

## Step 2: Choose your school, and say which

Read `references/brand-foundations.md`.

**Differentiation** (Ries and Trout) and **distinctiveness** (Sharp,
Ehrenberg-Bass) contradict each other and both are well evidenced.

- Considered, high-involvement, real functional difference → differentiation
- Habitual, low-involvement, near-parity products → distinctiveness

State which you are applying and why. Quietly mixing them produces positioning
nobody can act on.

## Step 3: Build the position

| Component | Requirement |
|---|---|
| For whom | Specific enough to exclude people |
| Against what | The real alternative, including inaction |
| The claim | One idea |
| Reason to believe | Evidence, not assertion |
| **The sacrifice** | What the brand is deliberately not for and not good at |

**The sacrifice test is the fastest diagnostic in brand work.** If the brand
gives up nothing, you have written a description. Push until something real is
excluded.

## Step 4: Read the category's codes

Positioning happens inside a system of existing meanings. Load
`references/semiotics.md`.

- What codes does the category already use, and which are residual, dominant, or
  emerging?
- Which **value positions are unoccupied**? Map the category on the four
  axiologies. Empty cells are candidates derived structurally rather than
  guessed. This is the strongest analytical contribution available here.
- Which of the brand's signs are indexical, meaning genuinely tied to it by
  contiguity and therefore hard to copy?

If the brand is luxury or premium, also load `references/luxury-codes.md`.
Restraint is not universally correct: some brands are built on loudness and
quieting them destroys what people bought.

## Step 5: Fluency diagnostic (when an asset feels off)

Use when the question is "why does this logo, headline, or asset feel wrong"
rather than "what should we stand for."

Fluency is about **decode speed**, not meaning. Slow decoding costs perceived
quality and authenticity.

1. State what the asset should communicate in one sentence.
2. Show it to five people outside the business for five seconds each.
3. Ask what they think it is, what it is for, and who it is for.
4. Score the gap between intended and actual.
5. Recommend accept or redesign against the evidence, not taste.

If real outsiders are unavailable, say so. Simulated panels are a thinking aid,
not evidence, and must be labelled as such.

## Step 6: Validate

Load `references/rubric-brand-strategy.md`. Score every line PASS or FAIL with
quoted evidence. Burden of proof on PASS. Fix, re-score, **maximum two rounds**.
State anything still failing.

Self-check against a written standard, not independent review.

## Step 7: Deliver

```
The position       for whom, against what, the claim, reason to believe
The sacrifice      what this brand is deliberately not
Why it is ownable  what makes it defensible
What changes       what the business does differently as a result
Open               assumptions made, what needs validating
```

The "what changes" line is not optional. A position that changes no behaviour is
decoration, and the team will read it as such.

## Boundaries

This skill does not own adjacent work. When the real problem sits
elsewhere, name the destination and stop rather than half-doing it here.

| If the real problem is | Route to |

| The work needs briefing before anyone makes it | `creative-brief` |

## Gotchas

- **Positioning that excludes nothing.** Reads well, forbids nothing, changes
  nothing.
- **Values presented as positioning.** "Integrity, innovation, excellence" is a
  culture statement. Every competitor claims the same three.
- **Rebrand instead of reposition.** New identity, same position, same problem.
- **Archetype as a substitute for strategy.** Archetypes are tone vocabulary.
  Picking one is not positioning.
- **Distinctive assets on a refresh cycle.** Changing them resets the
  recognition they exist to accumulate.
- **Brand valuation numbers as strategic input.** Methodologies vary widely and
  are often commissioned by interested parties. Track price premium and
  unprompted awareness instead.
- **Symbolism spotting instead of semiotic reading.** "Gold connotes luxury" and
  stop. A real reading names whose cultural competence decodes the sign and
  survives a commutation test.
