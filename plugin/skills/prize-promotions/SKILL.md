# Prize Promotions

A promotion that gives something away by chance is a regulated activity in most
markets, and a published one cannot be unpublished. This skill exists to put the
questions before the copy.

**This is not legal advice.** It tells you what to check and who to ask. Rules
change, differ by emirate and platform, and turn on facts only the operator
knows.

## Before anything: size the ask

A definitional question gets a direct answer. "Is a giveaway the same as a
sweepstake?" does not need the gate below.

The gate is for real promotions that will actually run.

## Step 1: Stop before drafting

> **Hard gate.** When the request involves a prize awarded by chance or contest,
> produce no post copy, no caption, and no terms until these are answered:
>
> 1. **Which market**, and for the UAE, which emirate and licence.
> 2. **Prize value and type.** Cash and in-kind are treated differently.
> 3. **Is a permit already held?** If not, when does this need to be live?
> 4. **Entry mechanic.** Follow, comment, tag, share, purchase, form fill.
> 5. **Where will the full terms be hosted?** A caption is not terms.
> 6. **Who draws the winner**, when, and who witnesses it.

The failure this prevents is sequencing, not ignorance. Delivering polished copy
first and the caveats last means the user can publish before reaching the
caveats. **The caveats are the deliverable.** Copy comes after.

If the answers show the timeline is impossible, say that plainly and early. A
promotion that needs a permit cannot go live tomorrow.

## Step 2: Check the market rules

Read `references/uae-promotions.md` when the promotion runs in the UAE or GCC.

For every market, three questions decide the shape:

- **Does it need a permit or registration**, and what is the lead time?
- **Is a purchase required to enter?** Requiring one converts a prize draw into
  something far more heavily regulated in many markets. "No purchase necessary"
  is a structural choice, not a line of small print.
- **Is skill or chance deciding the winner?** A judged competition and a random
  draw are different legal animals almost everywhere.

Never state a permit fee, a processing time, or a penalty figure you cannot
source. Name the issuing body and tell the user to confirm current terms with
them. See `references/evidence-and-proof.md`.

## Step 3: Check the platform rules

Every major platform has its own promotion policy, and breaching it risks the
account rather than a fine. The durable requirements on Meta platforms:

- **Official rules must exist and be linked.** Not summarised in a caption.
- **A release is required**, stating the promotion is not sponsored, endorsed or
  administered by the platform.
- **Eligibility must be stated**, including country and minimum age.
- **Do not encourage inaccurate tagging.** Asking someone to tag themselves in a
  photo they are not in is the prohibited pattern.

Asking people to follow, comment, or tag a friend in a comment is permitted on
Meta platforms. Do not tell a user otherwise — an incorrect prohibition costs
them a working mechanic for no reason.

Platform policies change more often than law. Treat this list as the shape of
what to verify, not as current text, and link the user to the live policy.

## Step 4: Then write

Once the gate is answered, produce three separate artifacts:

1. **The post copy**, in the brand's register.
2. **The short-form terms** that fit in a caption: who can enter, how, closing
   date, prize, and where the full terms live.
3. **The full terms**, hosted off-platform.

Full terms cover: eligibility and exclusions, entry period with timezone, entry
mechanic and limits, prize description and non-transferability, how and when the
winner is drawn and notified, what happens if they do not respond, data use and
retention, the platform release, and the promoter's full legal name and address.

## Step 5: Validate

Load `references/rubric-prize-promotions.md`. Score every line PASS or FAIL with
quoted evidence, burden of proof on PASS. Fix, re-score, **maximum two rounds**.

## Gotchas

- **Copy first, caveats last.** The documented failure. Answer the gate first.
- **Treating the caption as the terms.** It is not, on any platform.
- **Assuming "no purchase necessary" is boilerplate.** It changes what the
  promotion legally is.
- **Cash prizes.** Often restricted where in-kind prizes are not. Check before
  promising money.
- **Quoting a fee or a fine.** Name the issuing body instead.
- **Inventing a prohibition.** Telling a user a legal mechanic is banned costs
  them reach and credibility. If unsure, say what to verify and with whom.
- **Forgetting the draw witness.** Some regimes require an official present.
  That is a scheduling constraint, not a formality.
