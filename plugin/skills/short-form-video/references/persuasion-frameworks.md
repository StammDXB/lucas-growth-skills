# Lens: Persuasion Frameworks

Load when: writing anything intended to move someone to act, or constructing an
offer.

Claims are tagged by evidence quality. Do not upgrade a tag without a source.

## Route before you write

**Schwartz's two ladders** (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966) are a routing
decision made **before** any copy is drafted, not a checklist applied after.

- **Five awareness stages:** unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware,
  product-aware, most-aware.
- **Five sophistication stages:** how many competing claims the market has
  already heard.

The correct copy for a saturated stage-5 market is structurally different, not
louder. Most copy advice is stage-specific advice presented as universal law.

This is the single highest-value item in this lens, because it is the thing
general-purpose models most reliably get wrong. They default to
solution-aware, stage-2 copy regardless of the actual market state.

**Applied:** ask which stage the reader is at. If the brief does not say, ask
the user. Do not assume.

## Urgency has a mechanism, not just an ethic

The **Elaboration Likelihood Model** (Petty and Cacioppo) distinguishes central
and peripheral processing routes. This explains why scarcity works in impulse
contexts and is actively penalised in high-consideration ones: a motivated buyer
scrutinises the cue itself and reads it as manipulation.

Combined with **Friestad and Wright's persuasion knowledge model**, this turns
"do not use fake urgency" from a moral preference into a mechanism with a
predicted failure mode: the more motivated the buyer, the more the cue backfires.

## Evidence quality warnings

These matter because the field is full of confidently repeated folklore.

- **Virality.** Goel and Watts (*Management Science*, 2016) show most spread is
  broadcast-driven, not viral. "Make it go viral" is usually worse strategy than
  "reach a large node." The Salganik and Watts Music Lab experiment (*Science*,
  2006) directly falsifies the premise that content properties determine spread.
- **STEPPS** (Berger) is a post-hoc organising framework, not a validated causal
  model. Its primary study sampled the New York Times most-emailed list. Useful
  as vocabulary, not as prediction.
- **CRO statistics.** Most widely circulated conversion statistics have no
  traceable primary source. Do not cite a lift percentage unless you can name
  the study.
- **USP versus distinctiveness.** Reeves' Unique Selling Proposition and Sharp's
  distinctiveness thesis directly contradict each other, and both are
  well-evidenced. Sharp: "throw that Unique Selling Proposition out the door.
  Because customers don't care." Hold both and know which context governs.
  Do not resolve this tension by picking a winner.

## AI tells

The real tell is **sentence-length uniformity**, not any single punctuation mark.
The em-dash-as-AI-signature claim is weakly evidenced. Vary sentence length and
rhythm; that fixes more than any banned-word list.

## Do not cite as sourced

Checked and failed verification: the 60/40 brand-to-activation split (real and
IPA-sourced, revised to 62/38, but derived from self-selected award submissions
rather than a representative sample, so report both halves or neither); the
middle rungs of the Cannes Creative Effectiveness Ladder; "unique mechanism" as
a named framework (folk terminology, no identifiable originator); the ESOV
coefficient (conflicting secondary figures, primary unreachable).
