# Rubric: Offer Design

Score every line PASS or FAIL. No partial credit. Quote evidence. Burden of
proof is on PASS.

## A. Diagnosis

**A1. Market state is established.**
PASS requires a stated awareness level and sophistication stage, with reasoning.
Designing an offer without diagnosing the market is template-filling. FAIL.

**A2. The offer structure matches the market state.**
A plain claim in a saturated market, or identity positioning where the buyer
still does not know what the thing does, is a FAIL.

**A3. The buyer's alternative is named.**
What do they do if they do not buy? Including doing nothing. FAIL if unstated.

## B. Components

**B1. The promise is specific and in the buyer's terms.**
FAIL on category language, feature lists, or abstractions.

**B2. There is a mechanism.**
Why does this work when the previous thing did not? FAIL if the offer is a
promise with no explanation behind it, unless the market is genuinely stage 1.

**B3. Every component removes a named objection.**
List each component and the objection it removes. Any component with no
objection attached is padding. FAIL.

**B4. Risk reversal exists and is claimable.**
FAIL if there is no risk reversal in a considered purchase, or if the terms are
hidden, vague, or practically unclaimable.

**B5. The guarantee does not create adverse selection.**
Check who the guarantee attracts. If it selects for value-extractors, FAIL and
tie it to demonstrated use instead.

## C. Integrity

**C1. Urgency is real or absent.**
Any deadline, cap, or scarcity claim must have a stated mechanism. Manufactured
urgency is a FAIL regardless of expected conversion.

**C2. Urgency is calibrated to the buyer.**
Peripheral cues in a high-consideration purchase are a FAIL even when real, if
deployed as pressure rather than information.

**C3. No invented proof.**
Zero fabricated results, customers, testimonials, or numbers. One invented fact
fails the whole deliverable.

**C4. Nothing manipulative.**
No false stakes, no confirm-shaming, no hidden terms, no price anchoring against
a number that was never real.

## D. Consequences

**D1. Positioning impact is assessed.**
PASS requires an explicit statement of what this offer does to perceived tier if
it runs repeatedly. FAIL if only conversion was considered.

**D2. Delivery is possible.**
The offer does not promise capacity, speed, or scope the user said they do not
have. FAIL on offers that would break on fulfilment.

**D3. Assumptions are disclosed.**
Anything assumed rather than supplied appears in the delivery note.

## Scoring

Fix all FAILs, re-score. Maximum two rounds. Report anything still failing
plainly rather than looping or hiding it.
