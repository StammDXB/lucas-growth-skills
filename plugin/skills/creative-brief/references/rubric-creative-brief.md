# Rubric: Creative Brief

Score every line PASS or FAIL. No partial credit. Quote evidence. Burden of
proof is on PASS.

## A. The decisions

**A1. The problem is a business problem, not a deliverable.**
FAIL if the brief opens by naming an asset ("a video", "a landing page") rather
than the outcome it exists to produce.

**A2. There is exactly one proposition.**
FAIL if the brief carries two ideas, or hedges with "and" or "but" between them.

**A3. The proposition is internal, not copy.**
FAIL if it reads like a tagline.

**A4. The behaviour change is stated from-what-to-what.**
Sentiment shifts ("increase awareness", "build affinity") without a behaviour are
a FAIL unless awareness is genuinely the measured objective.

**A5. Success is defined before the work exists.**
FAIL if the brief has no measure, or a measure that could only be assessed after
seeing the output.

## B. Exclusion

**B1. The audience excludes people.**
FAIL on "anyone who", "all businesses", or a definition that rules nobody out.

**B2. There is an explicit out-of-scope section.**
FAIL if the brief permits everything.

**B3. No adjective hedging.**
Scan for "X but Y" constructions: premium but accessible, bold yet approachable,
modern but timeless. Each is a deferred decision. FAIL on any.

## C. Usability

**C1. Survives substitution.**
Put a competitor's name on it. If the brief still works, it is not briefing this
brand. FAIL.

**C2. Two good teams could produce different work from it.**
FAIL if only one obvious execution exists (it is a spec) or if any work at all
would satisfy it (it is too loose).

**C3. It can reject work.**
Name what you would point at to call the work off-brief. If nothing, FAIL.

**C4. Approvers are named.**
FAIL on unnamed approvers. Unnamed approvers are unsurfaced objections.

**C5. It fits one page.**
FAIL if it needs a second page. That is usually two briefs, or a brief plus
background that should be attached.

## D. Integrity

**D1. No invented context.**
Zero fabricated audience research, market data, competitor claims, or results.

**D2. Gaps are surfaced, not filled.**
If the user could not answer a required question, the brief says so rather than
inventing a plausible answer. FAIL on silently invented inputs.

**D3. The risk is named.**
The brief states the most likely way it produces the wrong work.

## Scoring

Fix all FAILs, re-score. Maximum two rounds. Report anything still failing
plainly rather than looping or hiding it.
