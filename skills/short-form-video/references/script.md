# Short-Form Script

Load when: writing the script, hook, voiceover, or on-screen copy for a vertical
short-form video.

## The three-second rule

The first second decides everything. Design **three simultaneous hooks** that
land at once:

- **Visual hook.** The single arresting image. Note it for the storyboard.
- **Verbal hook.** The spoken opener, or deliberate silence plus sound design.
- **Text hook.** A short on-screen line, added in post, roughly five words max.

Most weak reels have one of the three. The compounding is the point.

**Register matters more than volume.** In premium and considered categories the
hook stops the scroll through intrigue and beauty, not loudness. A sensory image
or a quiet confident line outperforms a punchy direct-response claim. In impulse
and mass categories the reverse is often true. Pick deliberately.

## Structures, mapped to seconds

Every beat gets a time window. Pick one; do not blend.

**A. Sensory immersion** (15 to 25s). Default for premium, hospitality, place,
craft, and anything where the feeling *is* the product.
```
[0-3s]    Hook: one sensory image + restrained line
[3-10s]   Immersion: deepen the feeling; one detail of craft
[10-18s]  The promise: the experience at its peak
[18-25s]  Quiet CTA: brand frame + one line
```

**B. Problem-solution** (15 to 30s). Service and utility angles. Use sparingly
in premium, where naming a friction can cheapen the frame.
```
[0-3s]    Hook: name the friction
[3-10s]   Why it matters
[10-25s]  The resolution
[25-30s]  CTA
```

**C. List or moments** (20 to 30s). "Three things most guests miss."
```
[0-3s]    Hook: the promise of the list
[3-25s]   One moment every 6 to 7s
[25-30s]  CTA
```

**D. Reveal or before-after** (15 to 25s). Transformation, or the reveal of a space.
```
[0-3s]    Hook: show the payoff first
[3-18s]   How you arrive at it
[18-25s]  CTA
```

## Hook patterns

Adapt to the brand's voice. Load `voice-and-tone.md` if a voice is defined.

- **Sensory.** Lead with the image in words. "Golden hour, then dinner under the stars."
- **Intrigue.** Imply what is not shown. "After the fires fade, the place is yours."
- **Stillness.** Slow the reader down deliberately. "A slow morning, as the city wakes."
- **Quiet value.** "Three things most people miss."
- **Reveal.** Open on the single most beautiful frame, then earn it.

**Avoid** the loud direct-response openers ("STOP scrolling", "You NEED this")
unless the brand genuinely lives there. They convert in some categories and
actively cheapen others.

## CTA

- **One action only.** Two CTAs turn a decision into a comparison.
- Quiet and confident beats urgent. "Reserve the season." "Plan your arrival."
- No urgency spam and no discount framing unless the brand strategy allows it.
  In premium, a discount CTA on a brand film undoes the film. See `luxury-codes.md`.
- The CTA can be a single on-screen line over the final brand frame.

## Voiceover versus silence

- VO is optional. A strong reel can run on sound design and on-screen lines alone.
- If used, keep it sparse: two or three short lines across the whole piece, not
  wall-to-wall narration. Write VO to **leave room for the visual**.
- Mark every line as **VO** (spoken) or **TEXT** (on-screen, added in post). The
  storyboard needs to know which is which.

## Output

```
# Script — [Title]

Hook (0-3s)
  VISUAL: [the arresting image]
  VO:     "[line]"
  TEXT:   "[≤5 words]"

[next beat] ([start]-[end]s)
  VO / TEXT: ...

CTA ([start]-[end]s)
  TEXT: "[one line]"

One-line rationale: [why this structure for this idea]
```

## Failure modes

1. **One hook, not three.** Visual carries it, or copy carries it, never both.
2. **Beat drift.** Beats not mapped to seconds, so the edit does not fit.
3. **Wall-to-wall VO.** No room for the visual to work.
4. **Two CTAs.**
5. **Wrong register.** Direct-response loudness on a premium brand, or precious restraint on an impulse product.
6. **Text hook too long.** Beyond about five words it will not be read at scroll speed.
7. **Hook that could open any video in the category.** The same substitution test as any other copy.
