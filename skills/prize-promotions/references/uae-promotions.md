## What triggers a permit in Dubai

Prize promotion campaigns run in Dubai require a permit from the **Department of
Economy and Tourism** (DET, the authority formerly operating as DED). This
applies to promotional campaigns offering prizes, whether by draw or as instant
prizes.

Reported conditions, all to be confirmed with DET:

- The applicant holds a **valid Dubai commercial licence** and has been trading
  under it for more than one month.
- **Fees vary** with the campaign: prize value, number of sites, and whether the
  mechanic is a draw or an instant prize.
- A **DET official attends the draw as official witness** for draw-based
  promotions. This is a scheduling dependency, not a formality.
- **Campaign duration is capped**, commonly at two months, with longer campaigns
  possible in defined circumstances and a draw required at intervals.
- **Cash prizes are generally not permitted** under a promotional permit.
  Benefits, incentives, in-kind prizes and vouchers are the expected forms.
- Certain **product categories cannot be promoted**, including tobacco, alcohol,
  pork products, and expired or spoiled food.

**The operational consequence:** a compliant Dubai prize promotion is not a
same-week activity. Permit lead time plus a witnessed draw date means the
campaign calendar is built backwards from the permit, not forwards from the
content calendar. When a user says "live tomorrow", that is the conflict to
raise first.

Sources, for the user to check directly rather than for you to quote:
Dubai DET's own promotions service pages; Al Tamimi & Company's published notes
on sales promotions in the UAE; DLA Piper's prize promotions country guide.

## Constraints that change the campaign design

These are design decisions, not paperwork, and they are cheapest to make before
anything is written:

- **Purchase as an entry condition.** Requiring a purchase changes what the
  promotion is in most regimes, and is treated more strictly than a free-entry
  draw. Decide deliberately.
- **Chance versus skill.** A random draw and a judged competition are different
  categories. A judging criterion that is not genuinely applied does not convert
  a draw into a competition.
- **Cross-border entry.** A promotion open to "the GCC" is several regimes at
  once, not one. Restrict eligibility to the markets actually cleared.
- **Free zones.** DIFC and ADGM operate distinct legal frameworks from onshore
  Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Where the promoter is licensed matters.

## Abu Dhabi and other emirates

Each emirate administers its own regime through its own economic department.
Abu Dhabi's requirements are administered separately from Dubai's and should not
be assumed equivalent. A promotion running across the country may need more than
one approval.

Do not generalise a Dubai answer to the UAE, or a UAE answer to the GCC. Saudi
Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman each differ.

## Electronic marketing to entrants

Entrants hand over personal data, and a promotion is a common way for a list to
be built. Two things follow:

- Entry into a promotion is not blanket consent to future marketing. If the
  entry form is also a subscription, say so at the point of entry and record it.
- The UAE's federal personal data protection framework and the TDRA's rules on
  electronic marketing both bear on what may be sent afterwards, in addition to
  any obligations from the entrant's own jurisdiction.

The full terms should state what entrant data is used for, how long it is kept,
and who it is shared with. See `references/evidence-and-proof.md` before quoting
any penalty figure from any of these regimes.

## What to verify rather than assert

Say "this requires a DET permit and here is what they will ask you" — that is
durable. Do not state:

- a specific permit fee
- a specific processing time
- a specific penalty for non-compliance
- that a given mechanic is definitively permitted or banned

Those are the facts that change, that vary by campaign, and that the user's own
compliance contact or the authority can answer in a phone call. Naming the
authority and the question is more useful than a number that may be wrong.
