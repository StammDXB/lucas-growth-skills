# Campaign Concept

You are developing the *idea*, not the artifacts. The idea is what generates
executions. If you produce executions and reverse-engineer a rationale, the work
will not survive its first channel change.

## Before anything: size the ask

If this is a quick question, a one-line lookup, a terminology check, or a small
task carrying no real stakes, answer it directly and stop. No clarifying
sequence, no rubric, no delivery note, no assumptions block.

The steps below are for real deliverables. Run them on a small question and you
produce ceremony instead of judgment, which is worse than a plain answer.

## Step 1: Clarify before generating

Ideas built on a thin brief are decoration. Do not generate until you can answer
these, and ask if the brief does not supply them.

Required:
1. **Who specifically**, and what do they currently believe or do?
2. **What behaviour must change**, and from what to what?
3. **What is true about this brand** that is not true about competitors?
4. **What is the actual constraint**: budget, channels, timeline, legal, category rules?
5. **What has already been tried**, and why did it not work?

Also worth asking:
6. Is this a launch, a relaunch, a defence, or a category creation? Each needs a
   different move.
7. What would the client refuse to do? The boundary is often where the idea is.

If the user cannot supply these, generate anyway but say plainly which inputs
you invented and that the territories are provisional. Never fabricate market
facts, audience research, or competitor claims.

## Step 2: Build in sequence

Read `references/campaign-craft.md` for the full apparatus.

**Insight → Proposition → Platform → Executions.**

Skipping to executions is the most common failure. The tell is a proposition
that describes the ad rather than the offer.

- **Insight** is a tension the audience recognises but has not articulated. Not
  an observation, not a demographic.
- **Proposition** is one internal sentence. Not copy. If it sounds like a
  tagline, it is doing the wrong job.
- **Platform** must be generative, ownable, durable, and livable.
- **Executions** come last.

## Step 3: Generate divergently, not abundantly

This is the step that most needs discipline, because the natural failure is
invisible.

AI-generated ideas are **not weak individually**. Research found LLM ideas judged
*more* novel than expert ideas. The failure is that the *set* collapses toward
one region of the solution space: many ideas, one underlying thought.

**So do not generate more. Generate under incompatible constraints.**

Fix three to five constraints that cannot all be satisfied at once, and produce
one territory under each:

- Only true for this brand and no competitor
- Works with no budget
- Only works with a large budget
- Starts from the product's biggest weakness
- A risk-averse client would reject it
- Requires the company to change something operationally

Then judge **variance across the set**, not the quality of the best one. If two
territories could merge without loss, you generated one idea twice. Replace it.

## Step 4: Pressure-test

Apply these before presenting. Kill your own work here rather than letting a
client do it.

- **Substitution.** Competitor's name on it. Still works? Not ownable.
- **Three-year test.** Could it still be running in three years?
- **Brief test.** Write a brief for a second execution from it. Cannot? It is an execution.
- **Operations test.** What changes on Monday? Nothing? It is decoration.
- **Disagreement test.** Could a smart colleague argue against it? If not, it is a category convention restated.

## Step 5: Validate

Load `references/rubric-campaign-concept.md` and score every line PASS or FAIL
with quoted evidence. Burden of proof is on PASS. Fix failures, re-score,
**maximum two rounds**. Anything still failing is a real constraint: say so.

This is a self-check against a written standard, not independent review.

## Step 6: Deliver

Present territories in a form a client can actually choose between.

```
For each territory:
  Name              short, memorable, internal
  The insight       the tension, in one sentence
  The proposition   what the brand offers against it
  Why it is ownable what makes it true for this brand only
  What it generates 3 executions across different channels
  The risk          what could go wrong, honestly

Then:
  Recommendation    which one and why
  Open              what you assumed, what needs validating
```

Lead with your recommendation. A menu with no point of view is not strategy.

Keep the delivery note short. Do not show the rubric scoring or the revision
rounds.

## Gotchas

- **Theme mistaken for platform.** "Innovation", "Togetherness", "Human". These
  are subjects, not ideas. They generate nothing because they exclude nothing.
- **Insight that flatters.** If the audience nods comfortably, it is an
  observation. Real insight is slightly uncomfortable.
- **The idea that only lives in the art.** If the concept collapses when
  described in words, it is an execution.
- **Convergent set.** Five territories that are one idea with different clothes.
  Check variance, not volume.
- **Borrowed cultural moment.** Attaching to a trend the brand has no right to.
  Fails the substitution and operations tests simultaneously.
- **Effectiveness folklore.** Do not claim awarded work is ineffective, or cite
  60/40, without reading `references/evidence-and-proof.md` first. Both are
  routinely misstated.
