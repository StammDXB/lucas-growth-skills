# Lens: Semiotics

Load when: reading or building meaning in a brand artifact, positioning against
category codes, or asked why something "feels" a certain way.

Every claim below is primary-source verified. Do not add unsourced material.

## The rule that governs everything

A connotation is not a property of the artifact. It is a **competence of a
specific audience.**

Barthes, analysing the Panzani pasta ad, found "Italianicity" signified by the
name's assonance plus tricolour yellow/green/red. Then the qualifier that most
people skip:

> "an Italian would barely perceive the connotation of the name... it is a
> specifically 'French' knowledge, based on a familiarity with certain tourist
> stereotypes."

Source: Barthes, "Rhetoric of the Image," *Image/Music/Text*, Hill and Wang,
1977, 33-37.

**Applied:** state the sign, the signified, and the cultural competence required
to link them. If you cannot name whose code you are reading, you are projecting,
not analysing.

## Core apparatus

**Icon / index / symbol** (Peirce). Icon resembles. Symbol is convention. Index
is "a relation of existential contiguity."

Index is the commercially useful one. Every authenticity, provenance and craft
claim runs on it: patina, a founder's hands, a workshop, a postcode, a date
stamp. It resists copying because a competitor lacks the actual contiguity.
When a brief asks for "authenticity," ask which indexical signs the brand can
legitimately own.

**Commutation test.** "Artificially changing an element in the advertising
message to observe whether the change modifies the meaning."

Swap one variable at a time. Serif to grotesque. Model fifteen years older.
Flint glass to amber. Golden hour to flat daylight. Elements whose substitution
does not change the signified are decoration. Elements whose substitution
collapses meaning are the brand's real semiotic assets. This turns "what do we
protect in the redesign" from opinion into a test.

**Paradigm / syntagm.** Paradigmatic = what could have been substituted.
Syntagmatic = what appears together in a chain (appetizer, entrée, dessert).
Marketing has over-researched the paradigmatic axis (positioning maps) and
largely ignored the syntagmatic. Usage-chain analysis is under-exploited.

**Floch's four axiologies:** utilitarian, utopian, critical, hedonic. Map which
the category already occupies; the unoccupied cells are positioning candidates
derived structurally rather than guessed. This is the strongest commercial
argument for the lens.

Sources: Christian Pinson, *Marketing Semiotics*, INSEAD working paper 98-39
(https://flora.insead.edu/fichiersti_wp/inseadwp1998/98-39.pdf). Floch,
*Sémiotique, Marketing et Communication*, P.U.F., 1990.

**Residual / dominant / emergent.** Codes sit on a time axis: lapsed, current,
or arriving. This is what makes a semiotic reading predictive rather than
descriptive. Source: Rachel Lawes, "De-mystifying Semiotics," *International
Journal of Market Research*, 2002.

## The four-part validity test

A reading is real only if all four hold. Treat as a blocking check.

1. The audience whose competence decodes it is named
2. It survives a commutation test
3. It is corroborated in an unrelated category
4. It is stated so another analyst working the same declared corpus could
   disagree with it

Cross-sector corroboration is Lawes' method: she established gold packaging as a
lapsed luxury signifier in biscuits by showing the same shift in premium bath
products. This is the discipline's answer to analyst projection.

Symbolism spotting fails all four. It says "gold connotes luxury" and stops.

## Gotchas

- **Projection.** Mistaking your own cultural competence for a universal one.
  The Italianicity failure in modern dress.
- **Opacity.** If the corpus and toolkit are not declared, the reading cannot be
  validated. Declare both, every time.
- **Jargon as false mastery.** The critique comes from inside the field: D.G.
  Mick titled a 1997 paper "Semiotics in Marketing and Consumer Research:
  Balderdash, Verity, Pleas."
- **Do not claim ROI.** The evidence does not support it. Documented academic
  readings exist; commercial cases are largely agency self-reports. The
  defensible claim is the Floch move: identifying which value positions a
  category has left vacant.

## Do not cite

Checked and failed verification: Cadbury, Guinness, Absolut, Johnnie Walker,
Bailey's, Dove as semiotics cases. Generic case-study content exists but either
does not mention semiotics or is post-hoc blog interpretation.
