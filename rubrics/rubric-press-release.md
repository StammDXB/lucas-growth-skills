# Rubric: Press Release

Score every line PASS or FAIL. No partial credit. Quote evidence. Burden of
proof is on PASS.

## A. Should this exist

**A1. Newsworthiness scored on at least two values.**
Impact, Timeliness, Proximity, Continuity, Prominence, Conflict, Novelty. Fewer
than two is a FAIL, and the correct response is to decline and route elsewhere.

**A2. Each claimed value names a specific external party.**
"Impact: the industry" is a FAIL. "Impact: 4,000 hospitality workers" passes.

**A3. If it is not news, the skill said so.**
FAIL if a release was written despite scoring below the gate.

## B. Structure

**B1. Lede is 25 to 30 words, 40 maximum.**
Count them. Over 40 is a FAIL.

**B2. Lede is active voice and leads with the most important W.**
FAIL on passive construction or on leading with the company.

**B3. Paragraph two is a nut graf.**
It states why this matters and why now. FAIL if the release goes from
announcement straight to quote.

**B4. Body descends in importance.**
An editor could cut from the bottom without losing anything essential.

**B5. Named media contact with direct line and timezone-stated window.**
A general enquiries address is a FAIL.

**B6. One page target, two pages maximum.**

## C. Quotes

**C1. Every quote survives the attribution swap.**
Put a competitor's name on it. If it still reads as true, FAIL.

**C2. Every quote is falsifiable.**
Contains a claim, number, causal assertion, or contestable judgment. FAIL on
quotes carrying only sentiment.

**C3. Zero banned terms.**
thrilled, excited, delighted, proud, game-changer, revolutionary, best-in-class,
world-class, leading provider, industry-leading, innovative, cutting-edge,
seamless, robust, empower, leverage, passionate about, committed to, unlock,
journey, "pleased to announce". Any instance is a FAIL.

**C4. Two quotes maximum, and not two internal executives.**

## D. Integrity

**D1. No invented facts.**
Zero fabricated figures, customers, quotes, results, or awards. One instance
fails the whole deliverable.

**D2. Every superlative is substantiated or cut.**
"First", "leading", "fastest-growing" need a source and a date. FAIL otherwise.

**D3. No material omission.**
Name what a sceptical reporter would ask. If the release is constructed so a
reader would be misled by what was left out, FAIL.

**D4. No SEO or backlink justification.**
Optimized anchor text in a distributed release is a stated Google policy
violation. FAIL on any link-equity rationale.

**D5. Embargo states a timezone.**
FAIL on "9am Tuesday" with no zone.

**D6. Vendor statistics are not cited as evidence.**
Journalist-preference percentages from pitch-software vendors are a FAIL unless
explicitly labelled as vendor-commissioned.

## Scoring

Fix all FAILs, re-score. Maximum two rounds. Report anything still failing
plainly.
